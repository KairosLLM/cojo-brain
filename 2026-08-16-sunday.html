Soft CPI, a rising long end, and the week the tape picked Ben on Bitcoin

2026-08-16 · sunday

Synthesized from what Benjamin Cowen and Jordi Visser published this week. They have not spoken to each other and did not participate. Quotes are verbatim and linked; everything else is labeled paraphrase.

Written August 19, 2026, covering material published August 10 through August 16 and market prices through August 16.

  • Ben: the S&P correction starts in mid-to-late September, and Bitcoin shows weakness first, trading below 60k before stocks roll. His August 15 memo put numbers on the whole thing: a 65% late-cycle base case.
  • Jordi: buy the basement trade. Gold and silver have moved, Bitcoin has not yet, and the dollar is already at new lows. The bond market will not rally even on soft data.
  • The tape: soft CPI crushed September hike odds to 24.5%, yet the 10-year broke higher and reaches 5.0% closed at 31%, the highest reading in the archive.
  • The record: Ben 1-0 with 12 open. Jordi 0-1 with 10 open. Scorecard

The pitch

Ben spent the week compressing two years of analogies into a dated, weighted forecast, and his written memo is the strongest thing he has produced all year. The August 15 report is more committed than anything in the videos. He puts the S&P at new all-time highs near 7,786, up 13.7% year to date, and names the mechanism that separates a correction from a recession:

"The labor market is what separates a correction from a recession, and it has not made the turn."

— Ben, August 15

He then states the whole thesis as a distribution, which is a first for him:

"The framework carries the view as a distribution: roughly 65 percent late-cycle base case, 20 percent deeper policy-error correction, and 15 percent recessionary transition."

— Ben, August 15

The valuation backdrop is the clearest he has stated: the Shiller CAPE at 41.2, second-richest in a century behind only 2000. And the call that ties Bitcoin to the equity move:

"its cycle low has historically formed during the equity correction rather than ahead of it"

— Ben, August 15

The August 16 video, The Winds of Winter, puts the date on the stock move:

"I don't really think that correction in stocks is going to happen until September, and probably not even until mid to late September. But normally, Bitcoin shows weakness before stocks do."

— Ben, August 16, 1:34

So Bitcoin breaks first, then stocks, then Bitcoin bottoms inside the equity correction. He is leaning on volatility compressing to a historic floor to time it. Sixty-day realized volatility is at 1.47%, 30-day at 1.16%, and he treats that as the trigger:

"within the next few weeks, volatility is likely going to pick up a lot."

— Ben, August 16, 10:50

The rest of the week is the same template restated. August 10 he called it crunch time and leaned down:

"I would lean towards breakdown because that's what it normally does near the end of midterm years, but only time will tell."

— Ben, August 10, 4:38

August 12 he put the seasonal ledger down, "on average Bitcoin drops about 10 to 11% in August and it drops about 8% in September," and the cycle low window in his usual words:

"I would say the odds the the low for Bitcoin based on the four-year cycle low, the odds are that it occurs between like late September and and and like mid December"

— Ben, August 12, 18:37

Paraphrasing his read of the next 60 days: the last three cycle lows landed on day 1432 and day 1436, Bitcoin is on day 1360, so the window is a few weeks out, which is why October remains his candidate month. His anchor:

"October to October makes the most amount of sense to me. If the market gives us to reason to pivot, I'll pivot."

— Ben, August 12, 20:09

August 14 was a product video, a new whale-watching dashboard, with one usable observation buried in it: whale transaction activity at the 100k, 1m and 10m size buckets is at levels not seen since August 2018, which he reads as confirmation of the low-interest, low-activity bear.

Jordi published his most ambitious paper of the year on Monday and his most quotable trade call on Sunday. The Monday Substack, The Yen Signal, argues that the late-July US-Japan coordinated yen intervention is the tell, a contextual signal rather than a single event:

"For the Fed and Treasury, we are running hot into a scarcity of tool options to fight long-term yields."

— Jordi, August 10

His frame for the long end, which is the exact observation that dominates the tape this week:

"Dollar Yen has become a new pressure point for the market to watch."

— Jordi, August 10

And the conclusion that names the trade:

"Gold's move this week may be the market's first acknowledgment that the solution to the debt problem will increasingly look like some form of debasement."

— Jordi, August 10

The Sunday video, AI Is Changing Time, opens with a headcount of the regime: S&P earnings up 51% year over year, sales up 15%, new all-time highs across the globe, his AI thematic portfolio up 3% on the week. On the Fed he is unambiguous:

"now we're down to 31% for September for Wor[s] and 80% for the BOJ. These numbers were about 5050 each before the payroll numbers."

— Jordi, August 16, 41:28

He calls the market's persistence wrong and names the trade:

"I happen to believe this is a mistake by the markets and you should be buying the the basement trade. You should be buying gold, buying silver, buying Bitcoin."

— Jordi, August 16, 41:28

The cleanest scoreable sentence in his week is the gap between gold and silver and Bitcoin:

"Gold has moved. Silver has moved. Bitcoin has not moved yet."

Paraphrasing what follows: the dollar is already down at new lows, gold and silver have responded to the debasement regime, and Bitcoin is the line still sitting at the bottom that has not yet caught up. He wraps it with the central-bank confirmation, China adding 20 tons of gold in July:

The synthesis

The two men's mechanisms converged on the long end this week, and the tape delivered for both of them. This is the rarest and most useful thing in the archive: an agreement, and a confirmed one. Ben's mechanism is that if the Fed does not hike, the long end breaks higher and does the Fed's tightening work, which is what corrects stocks. Jordi's mechanism is that the debt burden means the long end will not fall no matter what the data says, which forces the authorities toward debasement. Both predict the same thing: the 10-year yield rises. This week soft CPI landed and the 10-year went up anyway. That is the exact observation, and both of them described it in advance. Jordi said it most directly:

"We had a week where the inflation data surprised on the downside. Retail sales missed. We had a week before where the jobs number missed. And yet long-term yields are just not coming down."

— Jordi, August 16, 40:43

Hold that against Ben's rising-yields mechanism and you get the same event explained two ways that do not contradict each other. When that happens, it is worth saying plainly: this week they were on the same side of the bond market.

On the Fed they have quietly converged too, and it is a one-sided result. Jordi has been no-hike all along. Ben cited 57% for a September hike on August 4 and the number has collapsed to 24.5%. Jordi was right and Ben's number was wrong. But Ben's correction never needed the hike, and the long end has delivered the mechanism he did need. So the convergence on the long end is what lets Ben's core thesis survive his failed Fed call.

The disagreement is Bitcoin, and this week the crowd picked Ben. Ben wants a breakdown below 60k, Bitcoin showing weakness before stocks, and a cycle low formed inside the equity correction. Jordi wants the basement trade, with Bitcoin eventually catching up to gold and silver. Both cannot be right about the direction of the next three months. The market moved the whole Bitcoin ladder down this week, which is a bet with Ben and against Jordi. That is the clean head-to-head, and it is now priced.

The dollar is the one line they still hold exactly opposite, and it remains the cheapest disagreement on the board because nothing on the watchlist prices it. Ben says begrudgingly higher into year-end, Jordi says already broken down and fueling gold and silver and Bitcoin. Jordi repeated his side on Sunday. Both claims sit in the ledger and will be graded off the index directly.

The third voice

The long end breaking higher on soft CPI is the story of the week, and it lines up with both men. 10Y reaches 5.0% rose from 21.0% to 31.0%, and the biggest single-hour move was +9.0 at Monday 23:00Z, in the Asian session, the same pattern as the prior week. The 6.0% strike took +5.0 at Tuesday 04:00Z. Then CPI printed soft on Wednesday and the long end did not come down. That is the tension the whole week was built on, and it is exactly the mechanism both men had already named.

The Fed repriced toward Jordi. September 25bp hike fell from 35.5% to 24.5% and any hike in 2026 fell from 54.5% to 45.5%. Fed rate at end of 2026: 3.75%, the no-move-at-all outcome, rose from 34.8% to 40.1%. The crowd is now most of the way to Jordi's no-hike view.
