Midweek: the CPI print did less than Sunday expected, and the bond market did all the work

2026-08-13 · midweek

Synthesized from what Benjamin Cowen and Jordi Visser published this week. They have not spoken to each other and did not participate. Quotes are verbatim and linked; everything else is labeled paraphrase.

Midweek recap, covering Sunday afternoon through Thursday August 13, 11:00Z. It grades Sunday against the tape and opens no claims.

Sunday's week-ahead had six things on it. Two have resolved, one resolved in the opposite direction from the one this piece implied, and the one that mattered most was not on the list at all.

What Sunday got wrong

The long end broke higher on Monday night and Sunday never mentioned it. This is the miss.

Sunday's read was that the August 7 payroll print had knocked Ben's 10-year thesis back, and it said so: 10Y reaches 4.8% had fallen from 67.0% to 56.5%, and the claim behind it had "given a fifth of that back." Three sessions later the entire move is gone and then some.

Look at when it happened. The 5.0% strike took +9.0 in the hour at Monday 23:00Z, the 5.2% strike took +4.2 in the same hour, the 6.0% strike took +5.0 at Tuesday 04:00Z, and the 4.8% strike took +5.0 at Tuesday 08:00Z. Those are Asian session hours. Nothing American printed in that window. This was the Japanese long end pulling the Treasury curve with it, and it happened before CPI, not after.

10-year reaches 5.0% at 27.0% is the highest reading in the archive. It was 12.5% on July 26, 24.5% on August 2, 17.5% on August 9. Ben's most-repeated call of the year has never been better priced.

That matters twice over. It is the strongest week the tape has given 2026-07-28-cowen-04, and it is the mechanism that feeds his September correction: he has said all along that if the Fed does not hike, the long end goes instead. So far the long end is going without waiting to find out.

It is also, awkwardly for Sunday's framing, the thing Jordi wrote about on Monday. His paper is about exactly this pressure point:

"For the Fed and Treasury, we are running hot into a scarcity of tool options to fight long-term yields."

— Jordi, August 10

"Dollar Yen has become a new pressure point for the market to watch."

— Jordi, August 10

He published that Monday. The Treasury curve gapped in the Asian session Monday night. Sunday cast the yen intervention as the support beam under Jordi's no-hike case and left it there. The more interesting reading, visible three days later, is that Ben's rising-yields mechanism and Jordi's fiscal-trap mechanism are the same observation pointed at different conclusions, and this week the observation fired.

What Sunday got right, but smaller than it said

CPI landed Wednesday and it was not soft. It was in line with the hot tail cut off. Sunday's line was that a soft print "buries the September hike." Half of that happened.

The year-over-year ladder now reads 3.2% at 83.5%, 3.3% at 66.5%, 3.4% at 33.5%, 3.5% at 17.5%, 3.6% at 7.0%. That is a clean distribution centered just above 3.3%. What actually died on Wednesday was the upside: above 3.4% fell 17 points, above 3.5% fell 9, above 3.6% halved. The central case barely moved.

And the Fed barely moved with it. September 25bp hike went from 35.5% to 32.5%, three points, with -4.0 in the hour at Wednesday 13:00Z. Not a burial. A shrug.

The real Fed move was one meeting further out. Fed rate at end of 2026: 3.75%, which is the no-move-at-all outcome, went from 34.8% to 45.2%, and 4.0% fell from 35.5% to 23.2% with -7.2 at Wednesday 15:00Z. Meanwhile any hike in 2026 sat dead flat at 54.5% all week.

Hold those two side by side, because they do not fully agree. The crowd raised the odds the funds rate ends the year unchanged by ten points while leaving the odds of some hike this year untouched. Read charitably, that is the crowd moving a hike out of September and into a December that then gets reversed or never delivered. Read less charitably, one of the two markets has not caught up. Either way, Jordi's specific claim (2026-08-09-visser-03, no hike on September 16) is about the September meeting only, and it is getting better, not worse.

Worth naming what this does to Ben. He cited 57% on August 4 and 43.9% on August 8. It is 32.5% now. His September Fed call is the weakest thing he is carrying, and the correction he built on top of it does not depend on it, which he was careful to say at the time.

Bitcoin: Sunday's read was noise, and it retraced completely

Sunday said the Bitcoin distribution had "shifted up in a week both men called a bear market," and called it a modest bet against Ben's red August and with Jordi's breakout. All of it is gone.

The 70,000 line is back at 67.5%, exactly where it opened last week. The 60,000 line is back at 79.5%, within a point of where it opened last week. Three sessions erased a full week's move in both directions. Sunday treated a four-point drift as a signal. It was not one, and future weeks should want more than four points before calling a distribution shift.

Ben published twice into that and hardened. Monday:

"I would lean towards breakdown because that's what it normally does near the end of midterm years, but only time will tell."

— Ben, August 10, 4:38

"We are pre that event in my opinion."

— Ben, August 10, 3:51

Wednesday he put numbers on the window and restated the bottom range verbatim:

"on average Bitcoin drops about 10 to 11% in August and it drops about 8% in September"

— Ben, August 12, 3:05

"the odds the the low for Bitcoin based on the four-year cycle low, the odds are that it occurs between like late September and and and like mid December"

— Ben, August 12, 18:37

That is 2026-08-06-cowen-01 said again, six days later, in the same words. The claim is unchanged and better sourced than it was.

One open claim is offside and Ben pre-empted the objection himself. Bitcoin traded 63.5k Monday and 63k Wednesday, and it is up on the month so far, which puts 2026-07-26-cowen-02, the red-August call, on the wrong side with about eighteen days to run. He addressed it directly:

"you might say, well, right now, Bitcoin is actually up this month. But so what, right?"

— Ben, August 12, 2:19

Paraphrasing the rest: Bitcoin was also up in the first week or two of August 2022 and gave it all back into mid-September. That is a fair rejoinder and it is not a resolution. The claim is live, it is currently losing, and September 1 settles it.

He also opened a door he had not opened before, which is the scenario that would hurt him without ever falsifying anything:

"what if volatility dies out and we don't go up, we also don't go down."

— Ben, August 12, 16:16

His own answer is to hold the line and say when he would drop it:

"October to October makes the most amount of sense to me. If the market gives us to reason to pivot, I'll pivot."

— Ben, August 12, 20:09

Gold: Sunday's best observation is still standing

Sunday flagged that gold rallied 7.2% and Gold wins 2026 closed the week at exactly where it opened, and read that as the crowd calling it a rate move rather than a regime move. Five sessions on, gold is 19.5% to 19.0%. Still nothing. S&P wins 2026 is flat at 68.5% and Bitcoin slipped from 13.5% to 12.5%.

Jordi spent Monday's paper arguing the opposite, and it is the sharpest thing in it:

"Gold’s move this week may be the market’s first acknowledgment that the solution to the debt problem will increasingly look like some form of debasement."

— Jordi, August 10

The tape has now had five sessions to agree with him and has moved half a point the wrong way. That disagreement is unchanged and still the cheapest available expression of his thesis.

Two things not to read

The Kalshi growth ladder went incoherent on Wednesday and is not used here. Above 3.5% real GDP rose 5.0 points while above 3.0% fell 13.0 and above 4.0% fell 8.5, in the same hours. A single distribution cannot do that. The signature is the underlying event rolling to a new quarter rather than a repricing, so the growth and unemployment ladders are set aside this week rather than reported as a downgrade. Same for the August month-over-month CPI ladder, which is non-monotonic across strikes. The year-over-year CPI ladder above is clean and is the only inflation reading used.

The dollar is invisible to us. Sunday's closing bullet was that Ben and Jordi hold exactly opposite dollar claims. Nothing on the watchlist prices the dollar, so 2026-08-05-cowen-02 and 2026-08-09-visser-05 cannot be tracked between now and December from market data. They will have to be graded off the index directly. Flagging it now rather than discovering it on December 31.

Into the weekend

  • The Asian session. Monday and Tuesday's overnight hours moved the US long end more than the CPI print did. If that repeats, Ben's September correction arrives through the bond market rather than through the Fed, and it arrives earlier than his own calendar wants.
  • Bitcoin's month. Up so far, needs to be down by September 1, and Ben's own 2022 analog has the giveback starting right about now.
  • Whether the hike keeps leaking out of September. 32.5% and falling on the meeting, 54.5% and flat on the year. One of those two has to move.
  • Gold. Ben's gold low is in and Jordi's debasement thesis both want the same thing to happen. The cross-asset market wants neither. This is the widest gap between the two of them and the crowd on the board.
