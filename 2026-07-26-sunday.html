The calm week, and what both of them think is coming after it

2026-07-26 · sunday

Synthesized from what Benjamin Cowen and Jordi Visser published this week. They have not spoken to each other and did not participate. Quotes are verbatim and linked; everything else is labelled paraphrase.

Backfilled on 5 August 2026. This edition was not written contemporaneously. It uses only material published on or before 26 July and market prices as of that date, but it is not a live edition and should not be read as one. Jordi's Pomp appearance was imported by hand from a supplied transcript; its speaker labels come from diarization, verified against the host's own phrasing.

  • Ben: Bitcoin gets rejected at the bear market resistance band, and August and September are red.
  • Jordi: the easy-money phase of the AI trade is over, and the second half of the year is chop.
  • The tape: September hike odds jumped 17 points to 53.5% and neither of them led with it.
  • The record: week one. The ledger opens with four claims. Scorecard

The pitch

Both men opened on the same observation: this was a boring week. They took it in opposite directions.

Jordi said the blunt version on someone else's show. On Pomp's podcast the day before his own video, he opened with a flat declaration he never quite makes in his Sunday format:

"The AI trade's over. The ability of getting seven, eight times your money in that is over."

— Jordi on The Pomp Podcast, 25 July, 0:00

Read carefully, that is narrower than it sounds. He is retiring the multiple, not the theme. In the same breath he says he is focused on the second half of the year and on the agentic side becoming important for crypto. So: the easy money is gone, the trade is not.

Jordi's summary of the tape on his own channel is almost dismissive. The S&P finished down 61 basis points. The Nasdaq was down 1.5%. Small caps put in a fourth consecutive week of sub-1% absolute moves, which he flags as unusual in itself. Tech momentum bounced 8% after what he calls a gruesome four weeks.

His read on that bounce is the week's most forward-looking statement from either of them:

"After you have a massive move of this size with the V. The way I've shown it, I find very little chance that we go straight back up. If we did go back up, I think it's going to take some time. I think it'll take the whole second half of the year."

— Jordi, 26 July, 3:03

Paraphrasing the rest: he expects a period of chop where companies that beat, the Intels and Microns, find a bottom and build, while others fail to clear the bar. He read Google's quarter as the market fixating on negative free cash flow while ignoring a rising backlog and cloud growth. And he spent a large part of the video insisting the US-China AI competition is a genuine two-horse race, against people arguing otherwise.

Ben spent the week on the same chart he has been on all year: Bitcoin's bear market resistance band. Bitcoin traded 64-65k, opened the week at 64.7k and closed it essentially unchanged.

His frame is the midterm-year template, and it is specific:

"in both 2022 and 2018, and also in 2014, August and September were red"

— Ben, 26 July, 1:31

And his call:

"what I'm guessing as to how this will play out is that Bitcoin will likely get rejected around that area, just like it did in 2018, and then come back down in sort of the August to September time frame."

— Ben, 26 July, 3:02

Paraphrasing his supporting argument: 2018 is the best structural analog, the year has followed that playbook month by month, and Q4 is historically when the on-chain indicators reset. He also notes retail has not come back, using declining crypto YouTube viewership as his evidence, and treats that as confirmation the cycle topped on apathy rather than euphoria.

The backtrack

Nothing to grade. This is the first edition and the ledger starts empty. Both men are 0-for-0.

The synthesis

They agree on the shape of the next few months and disagree completely about why.

Ben's case is seasonal and mechanical. Midterm years do this. August and September are red. The template has held every month this year, so it will hold again. He is not really arguing about the economy, he is arguing that the calendar has a strong track record and that overcomplicating it has cost him before.

Jordi's case is structural. The momentum unwind of the previous four weeks was violent enough that a straight recovery is implausible, so the second half is chop while earnings sort winners from losers. He is not looking at a calendar at all.

Two very different methods pointing at the same window. That is worth flagging precisely because it is fragile: when two people reach the same conclusion from unrelated premises, one of them can be right for entirely the wrong reason, and the piece will not be able to tell which unless the claims are graded separately. They are, below.

Jordi's two registers are worth noting on their own. On Pomp he says the AI trade is over. On his own channel the same weekend he says a straight recovery is unlikely and the second half is chop. Those are the same view at different volumes, and the interview is the more useful one for a scorecard because it commits.

The genuine disagreement is about what the AI drawdown means. Ben barely mentions AI. His world is liquidity, seasonality, and the bond market. Jordi's entire frame is that AI is repricing terminal values and that this is a permanent change in market structure. If Ben is right, the coming weakness is a normal midterm-year correction that resets and resumes. If Jordi is right, something about the pricing of long-duration equity has changed and will not revert. Those imply very different recoveries.

The third voice

The Fed repriced hard toward a hike this week, and neither of them led with it. Polymarket's September meeting market moved from 36.5% to 53.5% on a 25bp increase, up 17 points, with a 5-point jump in a single hour on Monday at 16:00Z. The broader "any hike in 2026" market went 53.5% to 69.5%, up 16.

That is the largest move on the board and it went almost unremarked by both. Ben's macro attention this week was on Bitcoin's chart; Jordi's was on earnings and China. The crowd spent the week deciding the Fed is going to hike.

The long end moved with it. "10-year yield hits 4.8% before 2027" went from 21.0% to 36.5%, up 15.5, with an 8-point jump Thursday at 15:00Z. On the other side, "10-year gets down to 3.9%" collapsed from 26.7% to 10.1%. The market spent the week deleting the easing scenario.

Equities lost ground in the cross-asset market. "S&P wins 2026" fell 68.5% to 63.5%. Bitcoin was flat at 16.0%, gold flat at 18.0%. The crowd took 5 points off the S&P in a week Jordi described as quiet, which is a small hint that the quiet was not as benign as the index made it look.

One caveat on Bitcoin, stated rather than buried. "Bitcoin hits down to 60,000 before 2027" shows a move from 31.5% to 75.5% this week, including a 44.5-point jump in one hour on Tuesday. A 44-point hourly move on a market this size is far more likely to be a listing or liquidity artifact than a genuine repricing, so it is not treated as a signal here. The level, roughly 75%, is the usable number: the crowd broadly expects the lower move Ben is describing.

The week ahead

  • The FOMC on 29 July. The market has moved hard toward hawkish but neither man has said what he expects. This is the obvious gap in both their coverage going into next week.
  • Whether Bitcoin gets rejected at the bear market resistance band. Ben's call is explicit and the band is right above spot. The next two to six weeks test it.
  • Whether the momentum bounce continues. Jordi says a straight recovery is very unlikely and the second half is chop. An 8% bounce week is the start of the counter-evidence if it repeats.
  • The 10-year. Two weeks of violent repricing toward higher yields, with the market now at 36.5% for 4.8%. Ben has been calling this move for a long time without it happening. It is starting to happen.
