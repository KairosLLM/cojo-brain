The Fed blinked, the bond market didn't, and a $45bn fund went under

2026-08-02 · sunday

Synthesized from what Benjamin Cowen and Jordi Visser published this week. They have not spoken to each other and did not participate. Quotes are verbatim and linked; everything else is labelled paraphrase.

Backfilled on 5 August 2026. This edition was not written contemporaneously. It uses only material published on or before 2 August and market prices as of that date, but it is not a live edition and should not be read as one. Jordi's Pomp appearance was imported by hand from a supplied transcript; its speaker labels come from diarization, verified against the host's own phrasing.

  • Ben: called the 29 July hold a day ahead and says the freed long end takes the 10-year toward 5%.
  • Jordi: the worst four-day factor unwind on record was a cleansing event, not a beginning.
  • The tape: the 10-year-reaches-4.8% market repriced from 36.5% to 69.5% in five days.
  • The record: Ben 1-0 with 4 open. Jordi 0-0 with 5 open. Scorecard

The pitch

Ben called the Fed meeting the day before it happened, in public, with a mechanism attached. On 28 July:

"My guess is that the Fed will keep rates constant tomorrow, but raise rates likely in September."

— Ben, 28 July, 0:46

He did not stop at the call. He said what would follow from it:

"if the Fed does not hike tomorrow, which I don't think they will, it might be the perfect excuse for the 10-year yield to head back up to 5%"

— Ben, 28 July, 6:14

And he attached a size to the consequence:

"what I'm saying is I think we're about to see a 10 to 20% drop in stocks"

— Ben, 28 July, 9:19

The Fed held at 3.75% on 29 July. The next day he was explicit that the mechanism had fired:

"the bond vigilantes are revol[t]ing"

— Ben, 29 July, 1:34

His post-meeting reading, paraphrased: the 30-year has been rejected at 5.1-5.2% since October 2023 and is pressing it again, the 2-year yield is the best available proxy for the neutral rate, the 2-year overtook the fed funds rate back in March, and therefore policy is no longer restrictive even though nothing changed. He is blunt that the causality runs one way, that the market tells the Fed what to do rather than the reverse.

Jordi's week ended with something much larger than a Fed meeting. His 2 August video is about the late-July unwind, and his central claim is a diagnosis:

"I do think there's a high probability that this was a cleansing event."

— Jordi, 2 August, 0:01

The numbers behind it: the Goldman Sachs VIP index of crowded hedge fund positions was down 12% relative to the S&P for July, and

"Seven of the 12% occurred in the four days from Friday of last week until Wednesday of this week."

— Jordi, 2 August, 3:54

He calls that four-day move the worst on record, worse than COVID. Situational Awareness, reportedly $45bn of positions, took simultaneous margin calls, had its book marketed to buyers before Thursday's open, and was bought by Citadel with Millennium also bidding. Paraphrasing his conclusion: once the prime brokers are shopping your portfolio, the unwind is being administered rather than feared, and in his experience of LTCM, the 2007 quant unwind and Archegos, that is what the end looks like rather than the beginning.

On Pomp the day before, he put a number on the Fed — and it was not Ben's number. Talking about what made the week so violent:

"there's a lot of press that Citadel came out and said they'd raise rates. There were other people that thought they would raise rates. The probability was close to 40 percent."

— Jordi on The Pomp Podcast, 1 August, 6:25

He is describing the hike odds going into the July meeting rather than September, so it is not a like-for-like against Ben's 57%. But it establishes that both men were watching the same hawkish repricing and reading it at different intensities, and it is the closest thing to a Fed probability Jordi gives all week.

The same conversation produced his tightest statement of the structural case:

"hedge fund leverage was going up at a time when terminal value or being able to value a company in three years is going down."

— Jordi on The Pomp Podcast, 1 August, 9:54

That is the whole thesis in one sentence: leverage rising into falling confidence about terminal value, which is what turns a valuation problem into a liquidity event.

He also draws a structural conclusion on his own channel, which is the part that will be gradeable:

"until this volatility comes down and I don't expect it to come down quickly and I still believe structurally it's going to be higher than it's been in the past"

— Jordi, 2 August, 18:37

The backtrack

Four claims were open going into this week, all from the 26 July weekend.

Ben, 26 July: Bitcoin gets rejected at the bear market resistance band and trades down through August-September. Still open, horizon 30 September. Bitcoin ended this week around 63-64k, below the band, and has not broken above it. Nothing has falsified it and nothing has confirmed it. The market moved his way: "Bitcoin down to 60,000 before 2027" rose from 75.0% to 82.5% across the week. (2026-07-26-cowen-01)

Ben, 26 July: Bitcoin posts a red August. Still open, horizon 1 September. August had not started when the week closed. (2026-07-26-cowen-02)

Jordi, 26 July: no straight-line recovery in tech momentum; the second half is chop. Still open, horizon 31 December, and looking stronger. He said it after an 8% momentum bounce week. What followed was the worst four-day factor move on record. A bounce that immediately gave way to a historic unwind is exactly the not-a-V-shaped-recovery he described. (2026-07-26-visser-01)

Jordi, 25 July: the 7-8x phase of the AI trade is finished. Still open, horizon 31 December. Said on Pomp the day before his Sunday video. A week in which the most crowded AI positions produced the worst factor move on record is consistent with it, but one week proves nothing on a December horizon. (2026-07-25-visser-02)

Nothing is gradeable yet. Both are 0-for-0 with four claims running.

One call did resolve inside the week, and it was not in the ledger. Ben's 28 July prediction that the Fed would hold on 29 July and that the long end would revolt was made one day ahead and resolved the next. It landed on both legs. It is entered into the ledger this week and marked, rather than quietly credited, because a claim made and resolved inside a single week still belongs on the scorecard. (2026-07-28-cowen-03)

The synthesis

This is the week the two of them were reading genuinely different markets, and both were right about the thing they were watching.

Ben was watching the price of money. The Fed held, the long end sold off, and his mechanism worked exactly as described one day in advance.

Jordi was watching the plumbing. A $45bn fund was liquidated, factor volatility hit records, and the index barely moved. His whole point is that the S&P being flat was the least informative fact of the week.

They do not contradict each other here. They are describing two different transmission channels, and the interesting question is which one produces the drawdown Ben is forecasting. Ben's path is rates: yields rise, equities de-rate, the correction arrives in August or September on schedule. Jordi's path already fired and, on his reading, is now spent.

That produces the sharpest fork on the board. If Jordi is right that leverage has been forcibly reduced and prime brokers will not re-extend it, the mechanical fuel for Ben's 10-20% drawdown is thinner than it was in 2014, 2018 or 2022. You cannot get a violent unwind out of positions that have already been unwound. Ben's template does not account for this because it is a seasonal template, not a positioning one.

The counter, in Ben's favour: his correction does not require leveraged sellers. It requires the discount rate to rise. If the 10-year goes to 5%, equities de-rate whether or not hedge funds are carrying gross exposure.

Both men also converged, separately, on a hawkish September. Ben expects the hike. Jordi noted, almost in passing, that Citadel Securities publicly called for a surprise hike from Kevin Warsh and that this landed in the same week as hyperscaler earnings and the momentum unwind, which is what made the week so violent for crowded positions.

The third voice

The bond market did exactly what Ben said it would, and the size of the move is the story. "10-year yield reaches 4.8% before 2027" went from 36.5% to 69.5% across the week, up 33 points, including a 15-point jump in a single hour on Friday at 12:00Z. "Reaches 5.0%" doubled, from 12.5% to 24.5%.

Three weeks ago the crowd gave Ben's 10-year thesis a one-in-five chance. It now gives it better than two-in-three. He has been making this call for a long time without it paying, and it paid this week.

The Fed meeting itself is visible in the tape. "Any hike in 2026" fell from 70.5% to 67.5% on the week, but the interesting number is inside it: the largest single-hour move was -6.5 points at Wednesday 18:00Z, which is the FOMC decision. The crowd took hike odds down on the hold, then spent the rest of the week putting them back. The September market went the other way, 53.5% to 59.5%, with a 6-point jump Friday at 21:00Z.

So the market ended the week reading it the way Ben did: not this meeting, September instead.

"Zero cuts in 2026" rose to 88.5%. The easing cycle is, as far as the crowd is concerned, finished.

Equities recovered in the cross-asset market. "S&P wins 2026" went from 64.0% to 69.0%, reversing the prior week's fall. Bitcoin slipped to 14.5%. Notable because it happened during the worst factor unwind on record, which supports Jordi's framing that the damage was inside the market rather than to it.

The week ahead

  • Does the 10-year keep going? The market repriced 33 points toward Ben's thesis in five days. Either it continues toward 5% or this was the overshoot.
  • August, the month both men have flagged. Ben's red-August call for Bitcoin and his 10-20% equity drawdown both start their clock now.
  • Was the unwind really cleansing? Jordi's claim has a 31 October horizon. The test is whether crowded AI positions re-accumulate or whether the tape stays choppy. He predicts choppy and says leverage does not return.
  • 16 September. Ben expects the hike. The market is at 59.5% and rising. If both are right this becomes the first properly scoreable macro call of the archive.
  • Factor volatility. Jordi says realised vol on tech momentum stays structurally above its old highs near 70. It printed 110 this week. Mean reversion toward 70 would weaken his "changed forever" framing considerably.
